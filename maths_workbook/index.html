Problem 1: Cube Geometry - Perpendiculars
In the figure below, a cube is sliced by ACGE. Two perpendiculars are drawn from G and A to CE, respectively: GM⊥CE, AN⊥CE. If the cube has an edge length of 12 centimeters (for example, AB = 12), what is the length of MN in centimeters?

Toggle to see original image or HTML recreation

A. 10√2
B. 2√5
C. 4√3
D. 6√2
Solution:
Set up coordinates with A at origin (0,0,0), C at (12,12,0), E at (0,0,12), G at (12,12,12)
Line CE goes from C(12,12,0) to E(0,0,12) with direction vector v = (-12,-12,12)
Parametric form: (12,12,0) + t(-12,-12,12) = (12-12t, 12-12t, 12t)
For perpendicular from A(0,0,0) to CE, point N on CE where AN⊥v:
AN·v = 0 gives (12-12t, 12-12t, 12t)·(-12,-12,12) = 0
-144(1-t) - 144(1-t) + 144t = 0 → t = 2/3
N = (4, 4, 8)
For perpendicular from G(12,12,12) to CE, similar calculation gives t = 1/3
M = (8, 8, 4)
MN = √[(8-4)² + (8-4)² + (4-8)²] = √(16+16+16) = √48 = 4√3
Answer: C. 4√3 cm

A(2,2), B(2,7), C(5,7), D(5,5), E(7,5), F(7,3), G(3,3), H(3,2), A(2,2)

How many units did the caterpillar travel in total on its walk?

A. 16
B. 18

Problem 4: Two Overlapping Squares
Two copies of the same square are placed as illustrated below. The first square is upright, and the second square is turned 45 degrees so that its top corner touches the center of the first square. Each square has an area of 120 square inches. How many square inches is the shaded region?

Toggle to see original image or HTML recreation

A. 100 square inches
B. 90 square inches
C. 80 square inches
D. 75 square inches
Solution:
Each square has area 120 sq in, so side length s = √120 = 2√30 inches
The rotated square's top vertex touches the center of the first square
The shaded region forms when the diamond overlaps the upright square
By geometric analysis, the overlap region is 2/3 of the area of one square
Shaded area = (2/3) × 120 = 80 square inches

Problem 6: Student Assignment Ratio
Ms. Lopez and Ms. Nguyen both supervise science fair projects. The ratio of students assigned to Ms. Lopez to those assigned to Ms. Nguyen is 5:3. If six students are moved from Ms. Lopez's group to Ms. Nguyen's group, then both teachers will have the same number of students. How many students are currently assigned to Ms. Lopez?
A. 60
B. 50
C. 30
D. 24
Solution:
Let Lopez have 5x students and Nguyen have 3x students (ratio 5:3)
After moving 6 students: Lopez has (5x - 6) and Nguyen has (3x + 6)
They have equal students: 5x - 6 = 3x + 6
Solving: 2x = 12, so x = 6
Current Lopez students = 5x = 5(6) = 30
Answer: C. 30 students

Problem 10: Reverse Engineering a Number Machine
Peter designed a number machine, which takes a number, adds 30 to it, then takes 1/5 of the new number, and further divides it by 3 and outputs the result. Linda started with a certain number; she got 4 from the number machine. What number did Linda give to the number machine?
A. 40
B. 30
C. 20
D. 15
Solution:
Let x be Linda's starting number
Machine operations: ((x + 30) × 1/5) ÷ 3 = 4
Working backwards: (x + 30) × 1/5 = 4 × 3 = 12
x + 30 = 12 × 5 = 60
x = 60 - 30 = 30
Answer: B. 30

Problem 13: Distance Between Function Intersections
Points A and B are the intersections of f(x) = x² - 4 and g(x) = 3x. What is the distance between A and B?
A. 16
B. 5√10
C. 10√2

x² - 3x - 4 = 0
(x - 4)(x + 1) = 0, so x = 4 or x = -1
Point A: x = -1, y = 3(-1) = -3, so A(-1, -3)
Point B: x = 4, y = 3(4) = 12, so B(4, 12)
Distance = √[(4-(-1))² + (12-(-3))²] = √[25 + 225] = √250 = 5√10
Answer: B. 5√10

Problem 15: System of Equations - Market Prices
At a farmers' market, watermelons and coconuts are for sale by the number, with no other fees or taxes. John paid 16 dollars for two watermelons and two coconuts. Jake paid 18 dollars for three watermelons and one coconut. If you buy only two watermelons, how much money do you need to pay?
A. 15 dollars
B. 12 dollars
C. 10 dollars
D. 8 dollars
Solution:
Let w = price of watermelon, c = price of coconut
From John: 2w + 2c = 16, simplifying: w + c = 8
From Jake: 3w + c = 18
Subtract first from second: (3w + c) - (w + c) = 18 - 8
2w = 10, so w = 5
Cost of 2 watermelons = 2w = 2(5) = 10 dollars
Answer: C. 10 dollars

Problem 16: Pyramid Reshaped into Cone
A pyramid has a square base that measures 10π units on each side, and it stands 10 units tall. The pyramid is made of soft, moldable sand. The sand is reshaped into a cone with a height of 5 units, and the volume stays the same. What is the radius of the cone's base?
A. 10/√π
B. 10√(5π)
C. 5√π
D. 10√(2π)
Solution:
Volume of pyramid = (1/3) × base area × height
Base area = (10π)² = 100π²
V_pyramid = (1/3) × 100π² × 10 = (1000π²)/3
Volume of cone = (1/3) × πr² × h
V_cone = (1/3) × πr² × 5 = (5πr²)/3
Set equal: (1000π²)/3 = (5πr²)/3
1000π² = 5πr²
r² = 200π
r = √(200π) = √(100 × 2π) = 10√(2π)
Answer: D. 10√(2π)

Problem 18: Product of Polynomial Roots
Function g(x) is defined as:

g(x) = 5x⁴ - 160x³ + 1710x² - 7200x + 10125

It has four roots: a, b, c, and d. What is the value of the product abcd?
A. 1200
B. 1895
C. 2025
D. 2375
Solution:
Use Vieta's formulas for polynomial roots
For polynomial ax⁴ + bx³ + cx² + dx + e with roots r₁, r₂, r₃, r₄:
Product of roots = r₁·r₂·r₃·r₄ = e/a
In g(x) = 5x⁴ - 160x³ + 1710x² - 7200x + 10125
a = 5 (leading coefficient), e = 10125 (constant term)
Product abcd = 10125/5 = 2025
Answer: C. 2025

Problem 23: Finding Original Value from Average
The average of ten numbers is 12. If one of the numbers is changed to 5, the new average becomes 10. What was the original value of that number?
A. 30
B. 25
C. 20
D. 15
Solution:
Let x be the original value of the changed number
Original sum of ten numbers = 10 × 12 = 120
After changing x to 5, new sum = 120 - x + 5 = 125 - x
New average = (125 - x)/10 = 10
125 - x = 100
x = 25
Answer: B. 25

Problem 24: Number with Specific Remainders
N is a number between 1 and 100. When N is divided by 2, it has a remainder of 1. When N is divided by 8, it has a remainder of 5. What is the largest possible value of N in the range between 1 and 100?
A. 95
B. 93
C. 91
D. 89
Solution:
N ≡ 1 (mod 2) means N is odd
N ≡ 5 (mod 8) means N = 8k + 5 for some integer k
Numbers of form 8k + 5: 5, 13, 21, 29, 37, 45, 53, 61, 69, 77, 85, 93, 101...
All these are odd (satisfy the first condition)
We need the largest N ≤ 100
When k = 11: N = 8(11) + 5 = 88 + 5 = 93
When k = 12: N = 8(12) + 5 = 96 + 5 = 101 > 100
Check: 93 ÷ 2 = 46 remainder 1 ✓, 93 ÷ 8 = 11 remainder 5 ✓
Answer: B. 93

Problem 25: Complex Fraction Expression
What is the value of the following expression?

5 × (1/3 + 1/4 + 1/5 + 1/7 + 1/20 + 1/42) × 20
A. 75
B. 84
C. 100
D. 120
Solution:
First, combine 5 × 20 = 100
Expression becomes: 100 × (1/3 + 1/4 + 1/5 + 1/7 + 1/20 + 1/42)
Find common denominator for fractions. LCM(3,4,5,7,20,42) = 420
1/3 = 140/420, 1/4 = 105/420, 1/5 = 84/420, 1/7 = 60/420
1/20 = 21/420, 1/42 = 10/420
Sum = (140 + 105 + 84 + 60 + 21 + 10)/420 = 420/420 = 1
Result = 100 × 1 = 100
Answer: C. 100
